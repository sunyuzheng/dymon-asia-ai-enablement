# Dymon Asia AI Enablement Speaker Notes

These notes are written as a readable script. They are intentionally concise: use them as live wording, not as background notes.

## Slide 1 — The Third Interface For Investment Research

Good morning. Let me start with the purpose of this talk.

Portfolio managers have seen a lot of AI tools. Everyone knows AI is important. But for many people, the result is more confusion and anxiety, not less.

AI is amazing, but the work itself often has not changed much. Today we want to change that: not by introducing another tool, but by showing the right way to use AI as a new interface for work.

## Slide 2 — Opening Question

Let me start with a quick question.

Bill Gates wrote this in 2023, right after the launch of GPT-4. He put AI in the same category as only one earlier technology.

What do you think that earlier technology was? Most people guess the internet, mobile, or cloud. The fact that he said this so early makes the observation more interesting.

## Slide 3 — The Reveal

The answer is GUI: the graphical user interface.

GUI changed who could operate a computer. You no longer had to speak the machine's language.

AI is similar, but larger. Natural language now lets us command general-purpose work: search, write, analyze, execute, and connect tools.

## Slide 4 — PM Implication

Here is the one-sentence summary: with a new engine, we need a new workflow around it.

For PMs, this matters because the work is not just reading and writing. A PM coordinates context, makes judgment calls, delegates follow-up, and turns incomplete information into action.

AI becomes powerful when those moves can be specified, inspected, and reused, instead of repeated manually every day.

## Slide 5 — About Superlinear

A quick word on why we are teaching this.

Our vantage point is that we teach a very diverse, but frontier, group of people.

Our students range from developers at OpenAI to business operators to college students. The surprising thing is that all of them benefit when the training is built around real work instead of tool demos.

So rest assured: you are hearing this from actual builders who apply AI in practice and teach it to people in Silicon Valley.

## Slide 6 — Narrative Map

Here is the path for the session.

First, we frame AI as the third interface. Then we discuss why the operating model matters.

After that, we look at market signals, explain what this course trains, translate it into PM workflow, and use Yan's demo to make the pattern concrete.

## Slide 7 — Part I

Part one is about the dispatch layer.

Natural language is valuable only when it moves work. If it just produces text, the human still becomes the middleware.

The real question is: can language coordinate tools, context, artifacts, review, and memory?

## Slide 8 — The Third Interface

Think about the three interfaces.

GUI made specific tasks accessible. Programming is general, but requires technical fluency. Natural language is both low-barrier and general-purpose.

But the interface alone is not enough. Value comes from context, tool access, verification, and operating habits.

## Slide 9 — The Missing Operating Model

New technology often disappoints when we put it inside the old workflow.

That is the point of this slide. A new interface with an old operating model creates limited leverage.

The work has to be redesigned around what the technology is now able to do.

## Slide 10 — The Daily-Work Wedge

A common question from our non-technical students is: I know AI can build great software, but my job is not to build an app. So how do I benefit from AI?

The answer is daily work: automation and context. Meetings, notes, follow-ups, reviews, and handoffs are where actions get missed, opportunities disappear, and context leaks.

But like the factory story, the new workflow is hard to see when you have optimized your work around the old technology. That is where our training creates value.

## Slide 11 — Chat As Ceiling

Chat is useful, but it has a ceiling.

The user supplies context, watches the process, copies outputs, moves between tools, and remembers what happened last time.

So the system feels intelligent, but the workflow is still shallow. The human is still the pipeline.

At the simplest level, the action is this: replace chat with agents.

## Slide 12 — Agentic Operating Model

The agentic model changes the role of the human.

The loop is: specify, retrieve, act, inspect, and persist.

The human does not disappear. The human becomes the specifier, reviewer, and judge.

That is where the 10x leverage comes from: one person can coordinate more context, more tools, more artifacts, and more follow-through without personally becoming the bottleneck.

## Slide 13 — Market Signal

Now, let's look at some actual signals from the market.

These are not random company examples. Developers are the lead market because their work already has files, tools, tests, and review loops.

That makes coding the clearest place to observe agentic work before translating the pattern to PM work.

## Slide 14 — Cursor / Anysphere

Cursor is the speed signal.

The point is not that developer tools are hot. The point is that a high-value profession can move very quickly when AI becomes part of the daily work surface.

The market is pricing workflow capture, not just seat-based software.

## Slide 15 — Anthropic / Claude

Claude Code shows the operating pattern.

Strong users are not just asking for snippets. They specify the task, let the agent work inside the environment, inspect the diff, tighten tests, and preserve reusable patterns.

The new craft is not typing every line. It is controlling the loop.

## Slide 16 — Enterprise Signal

The second signal is that incumbents are acting too.

Goldman points to supervised agents in high-stakes work. Shopify makes AI proficiency part of resource allocation. Block is thinking about intelligence layers and operating systems.

The synthesis for Dymon is simple: copy the AI-native speed, but keep finance-grade review and discipline.

## Slide 17 — Course Capability

This is where the course comes in.

The goal is not AI awareness. Everyone already knows AI is important.

The goal is to close the gap between having access to the model and getting real leverage from AI in daily work.

## Slide 18 — Five Gaps

There are five gaps.

Output quality. Failure diagnosis. Full-task delegation. Compounding memory. And new capability.

Many smart people do not feel AI's full power because they do not have access to observe how AI-native industries actually use it.

The change is also compounding, organizational, and large, so it is hard to get started. But you do not close these gaps by introducing one more tool. You close them by changing how the work is done.

## Slide 19 — Verbs, Not Nouns

We teach verbs, not nouns.

Nouns are model names, tools, RAG, MCP, agents, and benchmarks. They are useful vocabulary, but they are not the craft.

The craft is specifying, decomposing, delegating, inspecting, testing, revising, documenting, and reusing.

## Slide 20 — AI Is The New Excel

Jay's analogy is: AI is the new Excel.

I think this analogy is great because it says three things at once.

AI is becoming table stakes. It is learnable by business people. And it is still not trivial.

Opening Excel is easy. Building a good model is hard. AI has the same shape.

## Slide 21 — Course Design

The course follows that learning curve.

Session one: run a complete work loop. Session two: package judgment as context. Session three: turn the workflow into infrastructure.

The goal is to move people from AI users to workflow builders.

## Slide 22 — Course Outcome

What does Dymon leave with?

Not just prompts. A starter axiom library, personal PM context files, reusable workflow skills, a context ledger pattern, team context architecture, and a support loop.

This will immediately save time, make people more productive, and capture important context.

But more importantly, it shifts mindset and habits. Those are more durable than any specific method or tool.

## Slide 23 — Part IV

Now let's apply the capability to PM work.

The scarce part of investment work is not access to public information.

The scarce part is filtering, mechanism, judgment, sizing, follow-through, and knowing when your view is actually different from consensus.

## Slide 24 — Investment Work, Mechanically

Mechanically, a PM turns information into a decision.

Information becomes signal. Signal becomes mechanism. Mechanism becomes judgment. Judgment becomes a position that can be monitored and revised.

AI that only summarizes information helps at the least scarce part of the chain.

## Slide 25 — Contrarian View

Good PMs get paid for the correct contrarian view.

Not disagreement for its own sake. A useful contrarian view is independent reasoning under pressure to conform.

And that view does not come from abstract intelligence alone. It comes from intelligence plus lived context, reasoning judgment, and taste.

## Slide 26 — Context Moves AI Up The Chain

This is why context matters so much.

One-off research can give you coverage: a comprehensive summary, sources, and consensus framing.

But context-aware workflow can move AI into judgment: what matters now, what your source hierarchy is, what failure modes you care about, and what would make an insight actionable.

## Slide 27 — Context Architecture

Context is the operating system of judgment.

Source hierarchy, captured context, world model, variant lens, and action standard all change the output.

The highest-value context often comes from meetings, calls, objections, follow-ups, and prior decisions. Those are exactly the things that leak every day unless the workflow captures them.

## Slide 28 — What To Watch In Yan's Demo

Yan's demo hides a lot of technical complexity, so it appears simple.

I would invite you to watch for three things. First, language dispatches tools. Second, context changes the output. Third, work leaves memory.

The strong point is not automation for its own sake. It is that context becomes reusable, so future AI can work with what the team has already learned.

## Slide 29 — Closing

The goal is context that compounds.

At the practical level, that means less time lost to coordination and fewer missed actions.

At the strategic level, it means the judgment context of each PM and the firm starts to accumulate. That is what makes future AI more useful.
