# Dymon Asia AI Enablement Speaker Notes

## Slide 1 — AI Enablement for Investment Research

Open with the practical frame: the session is about turning daily work into workflows that preserve judgment, move action forward, and compound context.

Transition: define the throughline.

## Slide 2 — Throughline

Use the leak metaphor. High-value work is often lost between the meeting and the next action, between a note and an owner, between repeated themes and a decision process.

Land the three leaks: actions, opportunities, and context.

## Slide 3 — What This Session Is For

Tell the room what they should evaluate by the end: whether AI can become a practical operating layer around work they already do.

Transition: preview the demo early so the deck feels concrete.

## Slide 4 — Demo Preview

Explain that Yan will run a simple internal onboarding process review demo. The point is not domain complexity. The point is that everyone recognizes meetings, action items, follow-up, and lost context.

Transition: briefly establish Superlinear's credibility.

## Slide 5 — About Superlinear

Do not over-explain your background. Let the visible proof points do the work: Cornell economics, Statsig experimentation, Superlinear AI Builders, course scale, operator testimonials.

Transition: show the roadmap.

## Slide 6 — Narrative Map

Walk through the five movements: operating shift, market signal, PM translation, live demo, course architecture.

Transition: start with the operating model.

## Slide 7 — Part I

Frame natural language as a way to dispatch work across tools, not just a way to receive answers.

Transition: use Gates to make the category shift less obvious.

## Slide 8 — Quick Question

Ask the room to guess the first revolutionary technology in Gates's framing. Let people sit with it briefly. The setup works because most people guess internet, mobile, or cloud.

Transition: reveal the answer.

## Slide 9 — The Reveal

The answer is GUI. The point: the breakthrough was not just more compute; it changed who could operate compute.

Bridge to AI: natural language is another interface shift.

## Slide 10 — The Third Interface

Use the table quickly. GUI made specific tasks accessible. Programming gave generality but required technical fluency. Natural language gives a low-barrier starting point for general-purpose work.

The caveat: value comes from context, tools, verification, and habits.

## Slide 11 — The Missing Operating Model

Use the Red Flag Act and factory analogies quickly. New technology expresses value only when work is redesigned around it.

Transition: identify the leaks in casual AI use.

## Slide 12 — Chat As Ceiling

Walk through follow-through, context supply, and memory. These happen in normal PM and analyst work every week.

Land the phrase: the human becomes middleware.

## Slide 13 — Agentic Operating Model

Describe the loop: specify, retrieve, act, inspect, persist. The demo will show all five steps in a simple workflow.

Transition: give evidence that this shift is already underway.

## Slide 14 — Part II

State the market signal in one sentence: leading teams are redesigning workflows; incumbents are learning how to govern them.

## Slide 15 — Cursor / Anysphere

Use the table as a speed signal. The point is that an AI workflow can become the daily environment for a high-value profession very quickly.

## Slide 16 — Anthropic / Claude

Emphasize mastery. Strong users delegate loops, review outputs, tighten tests, and preserve learnings. Weak users stay at the chat/autocomplete layer.

## Slide 17 — Verbs, Not Nouns

Contrast vocabulary with craft. RAG, MCP, agents, and model names are useful nouns. Capability lives in verbs: specify, retrieve, delegate, inspect, test, package, rerun.

## Slide 18 — Goldman Sachs

Position Goldman as an adoption signal, not a simple success story. Even regulated incumbents are moving from experimentation to supervised agentic work.

## Slide 19 — Copy The Right Pattern

State the synthesis: workflow speed from AI-native companies, governance discipline from finance.

## Slide 20 — Shopify And Block

Use this as operating-model proof. Shopify makes AI a management expectation; Block pushes toward intelligence layers and shared world models.

Transition: bring it back to PM work.

## Slide 21 — Part III

PM work is not mainly a text access problem. The scarce layers are filtering, mechanism, decision, sizing, and follow-through.

## Slide 22 — Investment Work, Mechanically

Walk left to right: information, signal, mechanism, judgment, action. Summarization helps at the least scarce layer; context and workflow help climb the chain.

## Slide 23 — Contrarian View

Keep the Naval point. Valuable contrarian thinking is independent reasoning under pressure to conform, not reflexive disagreement.

Land the three bullets: understand consensus, find the correct contrarian view, turn it into a tradeable decision.

## Slide 24 — Context Moves AI Up The Chain

Explain one-off research as useful coverage. Context-aware workflow moves toward judgment and action.

## Slide 25 — Context Architecture

Move through the rows quickly: source hierarchy, world model, variant lens, action standard.

Transition: now make it tangible with Yan's demo.

## Slide 26 — Part IV

Reset attention. The demo is intentionally simple because daily leakage is easiest to see in ordinary workflow.

## Slide 27 — Demo Setup

Yan should introduce the transcript in one sentence: "This is last week's internal onboarding process review meeting."

The first prompt is familiar: summarize and identify action items.

## Slide 28 — Demo Beat 1

Read Yan's instruction. Stress owner, counterpart, time window, meeting length, and background email.

The important observation is hands-off execution across calendar, email, and notification.

## Slide 29 — Demo Beat 2

This beat proves cross-document context. The one-pager needs the Tom onboarding case, ABC Capital KYC correspondence, and SOP 4.2.

Point out source links and the Google Doc/share/email sequence.

## Slide 30 — Demo Beat 3

Explain the context ledger: people, topics, source links, owners, derived artifacts, and status.

Use pivot examples: China energy context, Indonesian coal recurring across multiple people, and rows where no artifact exists.

## Slide 31 — Demo Standard

Judge captured work: actions, opportunities, context, hands-off execution, and transfer into Dymon workflows.

## Slide 32 — Part V

Yan's demo is the reference case. The course value is helping PMs build comparable workflows around their own research, meetings, notes, and delegation patterns.

## Slide 33 — Course Wedge

Start with one real workflow. Prove individual utility first, then package the useful pieces into reusable skills and context files.

## Slide 34 — Course Design

Session 1: complete work loop. Session 2: package judgment as context. Session 3: turn work into infrastructure.

## Slide 35 — PM AI Fluency Gaps

Walk the five gaps quickly. The most important ones for this audience are full-task delegation and compounding memory.

## Slide 36 — What Dymon Leaves With

Describe this as a first operating layer: axiom library, PM context files, reusable workflow skills, context ledger pattern, team context architecture, and support loop.

## Slide 37 — AI Is The New Excel

Use Jay's analogy to make the learning path obvious. Excel became table stakes, but real modeling required training and practice. AI has the same shape.

## Slide 38 — Closing

End on operational compounding: fewer missed actions, fewer missed opportunities, and a context system that compounds with daily work.
